# Track E — Consulting & Customer Discovery

## What these hours buy

34.0 h, 18.9% of the programme, and the second-largest allocation in it. What
they buy is a pipeline where none existed, built entirely from public sources.

A positioning and niche statement written in W01, which then determines every
prospect selected for the following eleven weeks. Fifty-six hand-selected
prospects — the first 24 researched manually, before the extraction agent
exists, and 32 assisted afterwards, so the agent's return is measured against a
real baseline rather than assumed. Fifty-two personalised cold sends, 15 written
by hand and 37 drafted with assistance, carrying 104 follow-ups between them. A
Stage-1 workflow rehearsal against a public company, documented end to end from
public information. Two workflow documentation artifacts. One return-on-effort
calculation built from a measured before-and-after baseline rather than an
estimate. Two scored automation opportunities. A qualification checklist and an
offer sketch.

Every one of those business artifacts carries an `evidence_source` tag reading
real or simulated. That tag is the mechanism that stops a substituted artifact
from quietly inflating the funnel, and it is why a simulated artifact honestly
labelled is a pass while a simulated one presented as real is a programme
failure.

## Entry competency

**Zero pipeline.** The user's own words are *"nothing, i gonna build it."* No
warm network, no case study, no referral path. That is a user-supplied fact at
`USI-03`, not an assumption the plan made for convenience, and `CM-15` carries
it as Awareness.

Everything downstream follows from it. This track teaches client acquisition
from a cold start; it does not teach delivery on the assumption that clients
exist. The distinction matters because a plan can satisfy every one of the
brief's fifteen Track E topics while quietly assuming a pipeline — scoping,
pricing, proposals and delivery all read perfectly well if you skip the question
of how a stranger with no portfolio gets a first conversation. That output would
tick every box and fail the brief, which is why the obligation is written as six
conditions a checker can evaluate rather than as a promise.

| Id | The condition | Satisfied by |
|---|---|---|
| `ZP-1` | At least four concepts flagged as acquisition-from-zero must also start in the first four weeks, so acquisition begins before any delivery work | five do: `C-087`, `C-088`, `C-089`, `C-093`, `C-095` |
| `ZP-2` | At least one exercise builds a prospect list from public sources only — no introduction, no referral, no existing contact | `D-w01-3`, `D-w02-3` |
| `ZP-3` | At least one exercise sends cold messages to strangers and records the outcome per touch, stating up front that most produce nothing | `D-w02-3`, `D-w05-3` |
| `ZP-4` | The absence of a case study is answered explicitly as an objection, with a named artifact that substitutes for it | `D-w03-3` and portfolio item `PF-07` |
| `ZP-5` | The funnel's conversion assumptions carry an explicit no-case-study penalty instead of using the vendor-average band unadjusted | the corrected reply-rate band |
| `ZP-6` | Agency subcontracting is positioned after a case study exists, not as a zero-to-one shortcut | `C-101` |

`ZP-1` is worth reading twice, because canon corrected itself there. An earlier
draft counted a concept whose first application is W06, which meant a checker
implementing the condition literally returned a failure while the surrounding
prose read as a pass. A condition advertised as mechanically checkable that has
two readings with opposite verdicts is worse than one that plainly fails. The
fix did not backdate anything or lower the threshold — it recognised that
running your first discovery interview and your first call from a standing start
is acquisition work, not delivery. A first-timer with no pipeline taking a call
is acquiring a client, not serving one.

**choosing a niche.** Niche selection *is* market selection, which is why W01
counts as a Track F reinforcement week before Track F has any hours. Small,
tightly-targeted lists are reported at roughly 5.8% reply against roughly 2.1%
for large-list sends — a self-selected comparison inside one vendor's own
customer base, so directional rather than predictive. It is quoted anyway
because it is the only lever a sender with no network actually controls.

**interviewing businesses.** A call that pitches earns a polite decline; a call
that asks a diagnostic question earns a workflow description you can put a
number on. The script is written before the first conversation for one reason:
without one in front of you, nerves resolve toward selling, and selling is what
ends the only conversation this prospect was ever going to give you.

**discovering repetitive workflows.** The skill is separable from the
relationship, and separating them is what makes progress possible from zero. You
can learn to document a workflow before anyone lets you near theirs. The
rehearsal artifact that produces is what you show instead of the case study you
do not have — which is also why it is load-bearing twice over and must never be
cut.

**identifying high-value automation.** Industry automation practice converges on
two axes, business value and feasibility, distilled from many sub-criteria. The
brief's nine dimensions map cleanly onto that pair, and the two it adds — can
you reach the decision-maker, and will they pay — are a real adaptation rather
than an oversight. Standard scoring frameworks assume the buyer is already in
the building; an outsider has to earn that access before any score means
anything.

**qualification.** A reply is not progress. Most of what lands in the inbox —
the courteous no, the unsubscribe, the assistant deflecting, the vague deferral
to next quarter — should move nothing on the board. Gate on pain the buyer
confirmed plus some signal about budget. Gate on how warm the message sounded
and you will keep mistaking good manners for momentum.

**ROI estimation.** The pattern buyers accept is hours saved per week, times a
*fully loaded* hourly cost — salary multiplied by roughly 1.3 to 1.5 for
benefits and overhead — times 52 weeks, minus platform and maintenance cost,
expressed as a payback period in months rather than a headline percentage.
Buyers discount unloaded estimates as naive and industry-average percentages as
vendor copy, because those are sector means no specific small business
recognises as its own. What persuades is a baseline you actually measured and an
arithmetic they can follow line by line.

**outreach.** The best-supported number in the whole market model: 58% of
replies come from the first touch and the remaining 42% from follow-ups, with
independent sources putting the follow-up share somewhere between 42% and 65%.
That makes two follow-ups per prospect close to necessary rather than optional,
and it sits inside the reported two-to-three sweet spot. Each follow-up must add
information — a touch that only checks in spends goodwill and buys nothing.

**discovery calls.** Handling speed matters more than almost anything else
available to you: replies handled within four hours are reported to convert
roughly twice as well as delayed handling, at low confidence. The operational
consequence is that the slot is reserved in advance rather than scheduled on
demand, because a reply waiting three days for a free evening has already lost
most of its value. Reserving capacity for a conversation you have not yet earned
is itself an acquisition discipline.

**producing a case study.** Case studies reduce buyer risk-perception
materially, which is exactly why their absence carries the reply-rate penalty
`ZP-5` insists on. The failure mode is an unverified time-savings claim, so the
tracking work must refuse to emit the artifact until a measured before-and-after
number exists. Published without one, it is worse than nothing.

## Priorities and what is deferred

The five P1 concepts share a precondition: none has a surface until an
engagement exists. Once the price is fixed, the only thing left that can move is
what you agreed to do, which makes scoping and pricing one decision rather than
two — and a pilot with its edges nailed down in writing is the difference
between a cheap first job and an open-ended favour you cannot withdraw from. A proposal leading with features loses to one leading with a
measured payback period. At this end of the market a defined outcome at a
defined price is what buyers want to say yes to, and billing by the hour is
defensible only where the work genuinely cannot be specified yet, capped at
moderate confidence in the low tens of hours. Every reference price band canon
carries came from vendor marketing, so they most likely describe what is asked
rather than what is paid, and they are recorded with that caveat rather than
quoted as fact. Writing the offer is P1 for a different reason: settling scope
on a quiet afternoon is how you avoid settling it live, mid-call, against
someone who negotiates for a living. Delivery of a first small project is P1
because Stage 2 exists to manufacture the one artifact Stage 1 cannot — a real
before-and-after number from someone else's business — and the case study, the
agency channel and any pricing confidence are all gated on it.

Subcontracting through automation agencies is P2, and the demotion is the
sharpest lesson in the track. White-label and overflow arrangements between
agencies and independent developers genuinely exist and are common. But every
source describing how you *get in* says it starts through a warm channel — a
conversation at a meetup, a referral, a message from someone who saw your work —
which is a cold-outreach-adjacent problem rather than a bypass of one. All you
have really done is change who you are cold-messaging, and agencies are the
harder audience, because they will not lend you their own client relationships
without seeing finished work first. It is therefore not faster from zero, and it
is homed after a case study exists. The durable lesson generalises: a channel that
requires proof of work to enter is not an alternative to building proof of work,
it is a consumer of it. Recognising that shape saves you from every future
shortcut whose entry condition is the thing you were trying to shortcut.

One structural rule governs how all of this is taught. The business half of this
programme is exercise-shaped, identically to the engineering half — every
substantial topic here carries the same seven parts as an engineering exercise,
objective through reflection questions, and a business topic with a deliverable
but no acceptance criteria or metrics is a defect rather than a stylistic
choice. Business content has a strong pull toward narrative playbook, and naming
the requirement is what resists it.

## How this track is proved

Track E is the only track that is primary in all twelve weeks. That is not
enthusiasm for business work; it is the funnel's lead time. Sends mature slowly,
follow-ups are spaced, and a week with no outreach is a hole in a curve that
cannot be filled later by doubling up — which is also why the compressed-week
subset in every week file protects the discovery floor and never doubles sends.

Proof runs on two clocks. The acquisition deliverables are early and cheap to
verify — `D-w01-3` positions, `D-w02-3` and `D-w05-3` send and record per touch,
`D-w03-3` produces the rehearsal artifact that stands in for the case study.
The judgement deliverables come later and are gated on other people:
`D-w06-4` and `D-w11-4` score and qualify, `D-w08-4` computes payback from a
measured baseline, and `D-m07-1` closes the first pilot. Nothing in the second
group can be manufactured by working harder, which is why the funnel's expected
outcomes are pre-announced rather than hoped for.
