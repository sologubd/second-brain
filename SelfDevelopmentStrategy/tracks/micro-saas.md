# Track F — Micro-SaaS Discovery

## What these hours buy

5.0 h, 2.8% of the programme — W10 1.5, W11 1.5, W12 2.0 — and the smallest
allocation anywhere in it. **That thinness is a decision, and it needs to be
read as one.**

Track F consumes evidence the rest of phase 1 is still in the middle of
producing: how often a pain recurs, whether anyone will pay to remove it, and
whether the people who have it can be reached at all. Those three inputs arrive
from discovery calls and workflow documentation across W04 to W09, and by W10
the funnel has produced only some of them. Build the validation framework before
that evidence exists and you get a scorecard scored on assumptions — which is
precisely the failure the kill criteria were written to catch. A framework
applied to invented pain will always return a favourable verdict, because the
person inventing the pain also chose the scores.

So phase 1 buys instruments and nothing else. A pain-scoring model derived from
the nine-dimension opportunity scorecard, built in W10. The seven evidence
thresholds with their open placeholders resolved to concrete numbers, each with
a one-line justification, in W11. And in W12 the five kill criteria applied to
the first candidate, producing a verdict — or an explicit non-verdict, which is
also a pass. Portfolio item #10 is the accumulated output. Months 05 to 09 are
where the instruments get pointed at something.

## Entry competency

Awareness, and `CM-16` is the slowest-moving row in
[the matrix](../reference/competency-matrix.md) by design. It reaches Working
knowledge at month 3 and stays there through month 6, arriving at Independent
implementation only at month 12.

That flatness is not pessimism about the learner. It is honesty about what gates
this competency: every meaningful input comes from other people — a buyer
volunteering that a problem costs them real money, three independent businesses
turning out to have the same problem, someone paying. Those are the one class of
input the programme cannot manufacture by working harder, and a matrix claiming
progress here on schedule would be claiming evidence its own arithmetic says may
not exist. Evidence: `D-w10-4`, `D-w11-4`, `D-w12-4`, and `D-m09-1` beyond phase
one.

## Concepts

Eleven concepts, seven at P0. Two are homed outside this file: the scoring
instrument belongs with the scorecard it derives from, and the kill criteria
belong with the validation framework that applies them. Rows give the priority,
the first week the concept has a surface, its home, and the deliverable that
proves it.

**problem discovery.** The brief bans *brainstorm SaaS ideas* outright, and the
ordering here is what makes the ban enforceable rather than aspirational.
Consulting comes first and product second, so the product hypothesis arrives as
a residue of pain you watched recur — never as a starting position you then
went looking for support for. The pain register is assembled from W04 to W09
calls and workflow documents, real or Stage-1 simulated, and every row carries
its `evidence_source` tag so the two can never be silently averaged together.

**pain scoring.** A scorecard re-derived each time it is used is not an
instrument; it is an opinion with a table drawn around it. What makes it an
instrument is that it repeats: score an opportunity, wait a month, score it
again, and the two runs should land in the same place unless something new
turned up in between. A gap you cannot attribute to fresh evidence is a defect
in the model, not a change in the opportunity. That repeatability is what W10
builds.

**frequency.** The multiplier in every payback calculation, and the single most
common place a plausible-sounding automation turns out worthless. A genuinely
painful task performed twice a year saves nothing anybody will pay for, and no
amount of severity in the description compensates for it.

**willingness to pay.** The first kill criterion is literally that people call
the problem annoying and will not pay to fix it. This is therefore the dimension
where wishful scoring is both most tempting and most fatal, which is why the
acceptance criterion demands a conversation or an observable signal rather than
a number you assigned. A score with no evidence behind it here invalidates
everything downstream of it.

**reachability.** This programme's own reply rate *is* a measurement of how
reachable the niche is by cold email, which makes the funnel dual-purpose: it is
business development and it is simultaneously the customer-acquisition-cost
input to the product decision. The consequence is uncomfortable and worth
stating plainly — a niche that will not answer email has an acquisition problem
no amount of product quality can fix.

**paid pilots.** Threshold four asks for at least one payment or a strong
purchase commitment. Money is the only evidence anywhere in this framework that
politeness cannot produce, which is why one instance is sufficient: it is a
categorical signal rather than a statistical one. It is also tracked as a
scoreboard metric at `SM-21`, so it cannot quietly go unrecorded.

**kill criteria.** The stated goal is learning to kill weak ideas early, and a
framework that always returns a score structurally cannot do that — a score is
always actionable in whichever direction its author already wanted. Making
*insufficient evidence, deferred* a passing output is what gives the framework
the ability to say no. It is also what protects the programme from inventing
evidence when the funnel underperforms, which under the corrected funnel is the
expected case rather than the pessimistic one.

### The chain, and why the order is the content

The brief teaches this track as an ordered process rather than a topic list.
Every link was homed somewhere, but the sequence itself was never written down
as a sequence — so it is enumerated here, because each link is gated on evidence
the previous one produced. Skipping a link is precisely how a plan arrives back
at *brainstorm SaaS ideas* through a side door.

| # | Link | What it produces |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | consulting and interviews | raw exposure to how a business actually works |
| 2 | recurring pain | the register: one problem shape seen more than once, across independent buyers |
| 3 | evidence | scored opportunities with per-dimension evidence, and the thresholds resolved to numbers |
| 4 | prototype | a concierge delivery, done by hand, before anything is automated |
| 5 | paid validation | money, or a strong purchase commitment |
| 6 | productization | a verdict — or a deferral that names what evidence is missing and when it could arrive |

A link may not be entered until the previous one has produced its artifact.
Phase 1 reaches link 3 at best; links 4 through 6 live in months 05 to 09 and
are contingent on the funnel. Saying plainly that the chain probably stalls at
link 3 is more useful than a plan written as though it will not.

## Priorities and what is deferred

The two P1 concepts are both kill instruments wearing other names. Competition
analysis exists to test the fifth criterion — that something already solves most
of the pain cheaply — which makes it an instrument for stopping rather than a
positioning exercise, and the difference in posture changes what you go looking
for. The concierge MVP is how the seventh threshold is met. Deliver the outcome by
hand, once, for one real customer first — because automating something you have
never successfully done yields a machine that reliably produces nothing.

The two P2 concepts are homed with their preconditions stated rather than
scheduled as if certain. A landing-page test measures whether a stranger will
surrender an email address, which is a weaker signal than the cold funnel this
programme already runs — its value is as a cheaper instrument for a question
already being answered, not as a new question, and it has no surface until a
positioned offer exists. Pre-selling is the one validation instrument enthusiasm
cannot fake, which is exactly why it is gated on evidence instead of scheduled;
stating its precondition means an unmet precondition reads as the gate working
rather than as the plan slipping.

Three things this file deliberately does not own. The seven evidence thresholds
and the five kill criteria are rendered in full by
[the validation framework](../business/saas-validation.md), which is their home
and the place their numbers are justified. The nine scored dimensions belong to
[the opportunity scorecard](../business/opportunity-scorecard.md). And the
consulting stages that feed all of this — in particular the fifth, where a pain
shared across enough independent buyers becomes a productization candidate — are
Track E's.

The non-verdict deserves its own statement, because it is the piece most likely
to be quietly dropped. *Insufficient evidence, deferred to month 05* is a
**passing deliverable**, on one condition. It has to point at the specific
threshold that came up short, and it has to commit to a month by which the
missing evidence could realistically be in hand. A deferral doing neither is not
a non-verdict; it is an absence wearing the word. And like every other
substantial topic in the business half of this programme, each of these carries
the full seven-part exercise shape — objective, task, constraints, deliverable,
acceptance criteria, metrics and reflection questions — identically to an
engineering exercise. Kill criteria and pain scoring are the most
playbook-prone content in the repository, and that shape is what keeps them from
drifting into narrative.

## How this track is proved

Track F is primary in three weeks and reinforces the nine before them, which is
the whole architecture of the track in one sentence. W01's niche selection *is*
market selection. W02 and W03 build a prospect list, which is market mapping.
W04 through W06 record frequency, willingness to pay and reachability from
discovery calls; W06 scores an opportunity on the very scorecard F later
consumes. W07 and W09 document workflows and their predictability, and W08's
payback calculation is a pain-valuation model. There is no gap across the twelve
weeks — F is being fed continuously and spends almost nothing.

Proof is therefore concentrated and cheap. `D-w10-4` produces the register and
the scoring model. `D-w11-4` resolves the thresholds to numbers with
justifications. `D-w12-4` applies the criteria and returns a verdict or a named
deferral. `D-m09-1` is where the later links close, if the funnel permits.

One reading of this track is that 5.0 h is not serious. The opposite is true:
these hours are small because the expensive part of validation is other people's
time and other people's money, and the programme refuses to simulate either. The
instruments are cheap to build. Earning the right to use them is what the other
175 hours are for.
