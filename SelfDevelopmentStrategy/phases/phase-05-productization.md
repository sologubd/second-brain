# Phase 05 — Productization

## Arc

Phase 05 spans [M09](../months/month-09.md), [M10](../months/month-10.md),
[M11](../months/month-11.md) and [M12](../months/month-12.md). It
is the longest phase and the least specified, and both of those are decisions.

Entering, Track F has produced instruments and no verdicts: a pain-scoring model,
seven evidence thresholds resolved to concrete numbers, five kill criteria. It
was thin through phase 01 on purpose, because it consumes evidence — pain
frequency, willingness to pay, reachability — that only the funnel and the
pilots can produce. Building the framework before the evidence exists yields a
scorecard scored on assumptions, which is the exact failure the kill criteria
exist to prevent.

Leaving, the instruments have been applied and a direction has been chosen on
evidence rather than preference. M09 runs Track F for real — competition
analysis as a kill instrument, a manual concierge build, a landing-page test,
and paid pilots or pre-sales where the evidence supports them — and populates
portfolio item PF-10 from the accumulated pain register. M10 stops building and
*operates*: the platform, the knowledge agent and the business agent run, and
the scoreboard is filled weekly from real runs rather than from intent. M11
audits the portfolio for credibility against a skeptical reader who has never
met the author. M12 makes the decision.

The thinness of these four months is a design position. Three canon deltas will
have rewritten the plan by the time this phase begins — hours against measured
actuals, the funnel against a measured reply rate, and the ecosystem against
re-verified sources — and the M03, M06 and M09 checkpoints each branch the path.
Specifying M11 in week-level detail today would be a fantasy schedule.

The calendar rule holds to the end: a missed month slips the schedule rather
than compressing two into one. In this phase that matters most for M10, whose
entire deliverable is *sustained* operation, and which cannot be caught up by
working harder in a later week.

## Entry conditions

- [ ] The pain register is populated from real discovery and pilot work, with
      every entry carrying its evidence-source tag.
- [ ] D-w10-4, D-w11-4 and D-w12-4 hold: the pain-scoring model, the seven
      evidence thresholds resolved to numbers, and a first verdict or explicit
      non-verdict already issued once.
- [ ] The consulting stage reached is stated plainly against its exit criteria
      rather than assumed from elapsed months.
- [ ] The M08 cut-list review is applied, so the remaining scope reflects what
      was actually cut.
- [ ] The systems from months 01 through 08 are runnable from a clean checkout,
      because M10 measures operation and cannot measure what will not start.

## Exit conditions

- [ ] D-m09-1 holds: competition analysis, a manual concierge build, a
      landing-page test, and paid pilots or pre-sales where the evidence
      supports them.
- [ ] D-m09-2 holds: PF-10, the opportunity scorecard, populated from the
      accumulated pain register — with a NO or an explicit deferral among its
      outputs, since a scorecard that has only produced yeses is a
      rationalisation engine.
- [ ] D-m10-1 holds: sustained operation with the scoreboard updated weekly from
      real runs, and business rows never simulated where money is concerned.
- [ ] D-m11-1 holds: all ten portfolio items complete, each with its credibility
      statement satisfied and each pointing at a runnable artifact rather than a
      description.
- [ ] D-m12-1 holds: the checkpoint decision with its evidence pack.
- [ ] The M09 SaaS verdict review, the M10 operating-evidence review, the M11
      portfolio credibility audit and the M12 year-two canon rewrite are all
      written.

## Checkpoints

Two of the four career checkpoints fall here.

**CP-M9** puts the end-to-end delivery question, against D-m07-1, D-m07-2 and
D-m09-1. It is the checkpoint most exposed to a weak funnel, and its evidence
requirement is deliberately not satisfiable by simulated work. If no pilot
landed, the honest answer is *not yet, and here is the measured reason* — with
the real reply rate and its denominator — rather than a demonstration dressed as
a result.

**CP-M12** is the four-way decision, against D-m09-2, D-m11-1 and D-m12-1. Its
question is held in [the portfolio file](../reference/portfolio.md); each option
carries its own bar. Consulting requires at least one Stage-3 paid engagement.
Productizing requires the same shape of work delivered twice. Micro-SaaS
requires all seven evidence thresholds met. The Staff or AI Engineer route
requires the ten portfolio items complete with their credibility statements
satisfied — and **it is a first-class outcome, not a consolation**, being the
only one of the four whose evidence this programme can produce unaided.

M12 also tests the nine closing design questions against the built systems. They
are listed, with the deliverable ids that make each answerable, in
[the difficulty ladder file](../reference/difficulty-ladder.md#closing-questions).
The weakest-evidenced of the nine is the business-value question, and it is
marked as such rather than quietly averaged in.

## Security arc

No new Track D topic is scheduled across these four months, and the arc closed
its planned depth at M06. What remains is operation: the least-privilege
profiles, the provenance audit log and the sandboxed execution surface are run
rather than extended, and M10's operating record is the evidence that they hold
outside a demo.

Two security-shaped obligations do fall here, both of them evidentiary rather
than technical. M11's credibility audit must confirm that the attack report
still reports measurements of this author's own systems with stated
denominators, and carries no borrowed industry effectiveness percentage. And the
repository is private now and public-capable later, so the audit is also the
point at which every tracked file is confirmed free of client names and prospect
data — real material stays in gitignored local files, which is itself a
discipline a buyer checks for.

## What this phase does not cover

Four brief topics land in these four months rather than going unassigned:
competition analysis, the manual concierge build, landing-page tests and
pre-sales, all at M09. Their specifics belong to
[the month file](../months/month-09.md) and the instruments to
[the opportunity scorecard](../business/opportunity-scorecard.md) and
[the validation thresholds](../business/saas-validation.md).

Nothing is homed forward from here, because there is no later phase. What this
phase does not resolve, the M12 canon rewrite carries into year two — and the
one thing it must not do is answer a question the evidence does not support.
An explicit non-verdict, naming the missing threshold and the date it could be
met, is a passing deliverable at every checkpoint in this file.

Also absent by ownership: the scoring dimensions, the kill criteria, the concept
inventory in [Track F](../tracks/micro-saas.md), and every hour and task, none
of which a phase file carries.
