# Month 07 — Evidence from someone else

## Outcome

A week belonging to someone outside this repository goes measurably better
because of something I built.

Every instrument up to here measures this system against its own baseline. That
is not worthless — a hundred killed replays and a frozen label set are real
evidence — but it is all self-scored. This month is the first time the number
comes from a process that was running before I arrived and is running
differently after.

This month is also deliberately less specified than months 01 through 06, and
the reason is structural: its central deliverable runs on a clock nobody in this
repository controls. A pilot arrives when a reply arrives, and week-level tasks
written against that would be a schedule for someone else's calendar. What
resolves the thinness is evidence rather than effort — once `D-m07-1` exists or
provably does not, the offer recalibration rewrites what follows from a measured
position.

## Deliverables

- [ ] `D-m07-1` — A completed Stage-2 pilot with a baseline measured before and after, written up as `PF-07`. Real names and figures stay in a gitignored local file; the tracked copy carries placeholders throughout, so the repository can be made public without a scrub.
- [ ] `D-m07-2` — The subcontracting side-quest: between three and five niche automation agencies approached, with what each of them asked for written down.
- [ ] `D-m07-3` — Paid pilots or pre-sales where the evidence carries them, and an explicit non-verdict where it does not.

The side-quest opens now and not earlier. Approaching an agency with nothing
built is a cold pitch wearing a different hat; approaching one with a delivered
pilot attached is a channel. The whole difference is `D-m07-1`, which is why
canon homes the two in the same month and why the target is small — five
contacts, not a second funnel.

## Funnel targets

Set by the M2 delta, like every month from 04 onward. No count is written here
because none was measured when this file was authored.

The number that matters this month is not a volume anyway. It is whether the
pilot's before-and-after was measured rather than estimated, and whether the
scope was fixed in writing before work started and stayed fixed. Those are the
stage's own exit criteria and no quantity of outreach substitutes for them.
Everything logged carries `evidence_source` as real or simulated, and this is
the one deliverable in the programme where the tag cannot be satisfied by
simulation: a case study needs an external party or it is a demonstration.

## Stages entered

None. `S9` remains open from month 05 and runs to month 08; no new stage of any
of the three systems begins here.

That is the shape of the month rather than an oversight. Consulting Stage 2 is
the thing being entered, and consulting stages are not project stages — they
progress on exit criteria and never on elapsed time, so a month can pass without
promoting one. Their definitions belong to
[Track E](../tracks/consulting.md) and the offer mechanics to
[the consulting offer file](../business/consulting-offer.md).

## Failure exercises

None assigned, and the arithmetic is closed rather than open. The fourteen
canonical exercises are spent one per week across the twelve detailed weeks, and
the extended set that months draw on holds five rows: two at month 04, two at
month 05, one at month 06. There is nothing left to allocate, so canon allocates
nothing instead of inventing a fifteenth to fill a section.

What replaces it here is not an exercise but an exposure. A pilot puts another
organisation's data inside boundaries drawn around this author's own corpus, and
the scope document doubles as a limit on how far anything can go wrong. A pilot
needing a tool surface the threat model never saw reopens it.

## Retrospective

All ten, and this is the first month where several of them have an external
answer rather than an internal one.

1. What can I now build that I could not build 30 days ago? "Deliver" is the verb this month, not "build".
2. Which concept remains theoretical? Anything the pilot did not touch stays theoretical, regardless of how well it is understood.
3. What broke in real usage? Someone else's usage, for the first time.
4. What did agents repeatedly fail at? Read against the pilot's own runs, not the demo corpus.
5. What should become a reusable skill? Whatever the pilot forced twice in four weeks.
6. What should become a deterministic tool instead of an LLM decision? Scope questions are the candidate; a model that negotiates scope has already exceeded it.
7. Where did human approval prove necessary? A client's data raises this bar, not lowers it.
8. What business problems appeared repeatedly? The agencies in `D-m07-2` answer this from outside the funnel.
9. What should I stop learning? Answered against the low-ROI table. A pilot is an unusually good source for this, because it shows what a buyer never once asked about.
10. What should I double down on? Its follow-up asks which of the last four weeks' deliverables taught you something you did not already know. A track scoring zero of four gets re-pitched, and delivery months are where that risk sits.

`RQ-11` converts one of those answers into an edit. The route runs
`make delta MONTH=07` first, then a canon amendment, a version bump and a clean
re-check before the month is called finished.

## Mandated delta

**Type:** `offer_recalibration`.

Canon's procedure: read the Stage-2 pilot's measured before-and-after baseline
and the reply data behind it. Rewrite the `consulting_stages` exit criteria the
pilot proved wrong, and re-price the offer against hours actually spent rather
than hours quoted. Then bump `meta.version`, regenerate, re-check.

The re-pricing clause is the sharp one. The offer written at `D-m06-3` states a
fixed scope and a payback period, both computed before anybody had delivered
against them. `D-m07-1` supplies the first figures drawn from hours that were
really spent. Where the two disagree the offer is wrong, and this delta is what
records that in canon rather than in a note to self.

## Checkpoint

This month sits between gates. `CP-M9` is the one it feeds, three months out,
and what it asks is whether an automation problem belonging to a real company
can be carried the whole way through.

Four deliverable ids answer it and this month supplies two of them. `D-m07-1` is
the pilot itself; `D-m07-2` is the agency channel and what it revealed. The
remaining pair, `D-m09-1` and `D-m09-2`, fall at the gate month.

Canon is explicit that this checkpoint is the one most exposed to a weak funnel,
and that its evidence cannot be satisfied by simulated work. If no pilot lands,
the answer at `CP-M9` is that it has not happened yet, stated with the measured
reply rate and its denominator. Siting the gate at month 09 rather than here is
what gives a pilot begun in this month a full quarter to finish.
