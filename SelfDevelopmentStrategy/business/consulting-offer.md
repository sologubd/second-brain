# Consulting offer — qualification, ROI method and the five stages

## Purpose

This file owns the qualification checklist, the ROI method and the shape of the
offer. All three are written long before they are needed, because scope decided
in advance is scope not decided mid-sentence with a buyer waiting — and a first
conversation improvised against nothing written is how a fixed price turns into
an open-ended engagement.

Everything here is contingent on a funnel that may not deliver, and that is
stated up front rather than discovered. Stages are entered on evidence, never on
elapsed time, and one whose exit criteria are unmet stays unexited however many
months pass. The calendar does not promote.

## The instrument

### The five stages and their exits

| Stage | What it is | Exits when | First reachable |
|---|---|---|---|
| CS-1 | Simulated: internal demos against realistic workflows | A workflow is documented end to end from public information with named steps, estimated frequency and estimated time cost; a discovery script exists and has been rehearsed; and the document stands on its own, in front of someone who has never met you, as proof that you can document a workflow | W03 |
| CS-2 | Free or cheap pilot, strictly scoped | A real external party's process is measurably different afterwards; the before/after baseline was MEASURED, not estimated; the scope was fixed in writing before work started and was not exceeded | M05–M07, contingent |
| CS-3 | Paid fixed-scope automation | Money changed hands; the delivered outcome matches a written scope; a payback period in months can be stated and defended | M07–M09, contingent |
| CS-4 | Repeatable offer | The same shape of work has been delivered at least twice; a fixed-scope, fixed-price description exists that a buyer can accept without a bespoke proposal | M08 onward, contingent |
| CS-5 | Productization candidate | The pain register shows one shape of pain across at least the number of independent buyers set in the SaaS thresholds, and all seven evidence thresholds are met — or the framework returns an explicit non-verdict | M09 onward, contingent |

CS-1 is the load-bearing one. Under the corrected funnel it is the *expected*
evidence path for at least one business deliverable in W07, W08 and W09, not a
contingency, which is why it must never be cut.

### Qualification checklist

Applied before the offer sketch, and applied to a reply rather than to a
feeling. Replies arrive in several unhelpful shapes — a courteous no, an
unsubscribe, a gatekeeper's deflection, a "circle back in the autumn" — and none
of them is a booked call. Qualification that gates on how warm a reply *sounds*
will keep mistaking politeness for movement.

1. A named person confirmed a specific process is painful, in their words.
2. That person can describe the process's frequency without being prompted.
3. Someone has quantified, or can quantify, what the process currently costs.
4. There is a budget signal — a number, an existing tool spend, a stated
   approval path — not merely enthusiasm.
5. The systems involved can actually be accessed, and this person can grant it.
6. Scope can be written in one paragraph a buyer would sign.

Fewer than four of six answered means the next action is another conversation,
not an offer. Item 4 is the one most often skipped and it is the one kill
criterion KC-1 is written about.

### ROI method

The pattern buyers accept, and the only one used here:

```text
time saved (hours/week)
  x fully loaded hourly cost        (salary x roughly 1.3–1.5 for benefits
                                     and overhead)
  x 52 weeks
  - platform cost - maintenance cost
  = annual net, expressed as a PAYBACK PERIOD IN MONTHS
```

Two failure modes are designed out. Buyers treat unloaded-cost estimates as
naive, so the 1.3–1.5 multiplier is not optional. And they treat industry-average
ROI percentages as vendor copy, since such figures are sector means that no
particular small business sees itself in — so no percentage is quoted, ever.
What earns belief is a baseline that was measured and a method shown in full,
not a large number.

### Offer structure

Fixed scope, fixed price, one outcome, one paragraph. Hourly billing is
defensible only for genuinely undefined discovery work, capped at 10 to 20 hours
when used at all. Once the price is fixed, the one thing still free to move is
scope, which makes scoping and pricing a single act — and holding it tight is
the difference between a cheap pilot you can afford to finish and one that
quietly becomes free labour.

### The agency side-quest

Subcontracting through automation and AI agencies is real and common, and it is
positioned here as a Stage 2/3 side-quest at months 05 through 07 — after a
pilot case study exists — rather than as a zero-to-one shortcut. The reason is
structural. Every source describing how you get in says entry runs through warm
channels, and an agency wants proof of work before putting its own client
relationships behind you. The route swaps one cold audience for a harder one,
and what it demands at the door is the artifact you hoped to skip. A channel
that consumes proof of work is no alternative to building it. The target is
deliberately small: contact 3 to 5 niche agencies and record what they ask for.

## Exercises

### Exercise 1 — payback from a measured baseline

#### Objective

Produce a number a buyer would defend on your behalf, from a baseline that was
measured rather than assumed.

#### Task

Take the W07 workflow document, measure or source the current time cost per
occurrence, apply the ROI method above, and express the result as a payback
period in months with every input and its source shown.

#### Constraints

Fully loaded cost only. No industry-average percentage anywhere in the output.
Every input carries its source; an input that had to be estimated is labelled as
an estimate with the basis stated. If the baseline is a Stage-1 simulated one,
the artifact says so.

#### Deliverable

D-w08-4 — the ROI calculation with the measurement method shown, tagged
`evidence_source: real | simulated`.

#### Acceptance criteria

- The payback period is stated in months, and every one of the 4 inputs — hours saved, loaded rate, platform cost, maintenance cost — is present with a source.
- The loaded multiplier used falls in the 1.3 to 1.5 range, and the document states which figure was used and why.
- 0 industry-average ROI percentages appear in the artifact.
- A reader can recompute the payback figure from the document alone, with 0 questions asked.

#### Metrics

Payback period in months. Sensitivity = the payback figure recomputed at half
the assumed hours saved, reported beside the headline, because a number that
survives halving is a different claim from one that does not.

#### Reflection questions

1. Which single input would move the payback period most, and how confident are
   you in it?
2. What would the buyer's finance person ask first, and does the document answer
   it without a conversation?
3. If this baseline is simulated, what exactly would change in the method once a
   real one exists — and what would not change at all?

### Exercise 2 — the offer sketch

#### Objective

Write the scope decision down before the pressure of a real conversation makes
it for you.

#### Task

Run the qualification checklist against the pain register, pick the highest
qualified pain, and write a one-paragraph fixed-scope, fixed-price offer with an
explicit exclusions list.

#### Constraints

One outcome, not a set of features. Exclusions are written before the price. No
hourly rate unless the work is genuinely undefined discovery, and then capped.
Placeholder identities throughout the tracked copy.

#### Deliverable

D-w11-4 — the offer sketch from the qualified pain register, which becomes
portfolio item PF-08, the consulting offer page.

#### Acceptance criteria

- The qualification checklist is answered on all 6 items for the chosen pain, with 4 or more answered yes.
- The offer names exactly 1 outcome and lists at least 3 explicit exclusions.
- The price is fixed, or the hourly arrangement states a cap of at most 20 hours.
- The scope paragraph is short enough that a buyer could accept it without a bespoke proposal, which is CS-4's exit test applied early.

#### Metrics

Number of qualified pains, against the register's total — the ratio matters more
than the count, because a register where everything qualifies is a register that
is not being read critically.

#### Reflection questions

1. Which exclusion would you have forgotten if you had written this during a
   call rather than before one?
2. What would you have to see to raise this price, and what would you have to
   see to walk away entirely?
3. Which qualification item did you most want to mark yes on thin evidence, and
   what does that tell you about your own bias?

## Targets and thresholds

Pricing reference bands, all from vendor-marketing sources and therefore likely
to reflect asking rates rather than realised ones: fixed projects cluster at
EUR/USD 5,000 to 25,000, with 10,000 to 15,000 typical for a four-to-six week
build. Buyers at this size reach for a fixed or productized price attached to a
defined outcome. Treat these as orientation, not as a rate card — this programme has no
realised transaction to calibrate them against, and will not until CS-3.

Phase 1 reaches CS-1 and no further, and that is the plan rather than a
shortfall. The programme's own targets through week twelve are one offer sketch,
one ROI calculation and 3 to 5 agencies contacted if and only if a pilot case
study exists first. Everything at CS-2 and beyond is contingent on a funnel
whose modal outcome is zero calls.

The case study has its own refusal rule, and it is the strictest thing in this
file: pilot tracking must refuse to produce a case-study artifact until a
measured before/after number exists. An unverified time-savings claim is worse
than no case study, because it is the one document a buyer will check.

## Evidence discipline

Every artifact here carries `evidence_source: real | simulated` on its
scoreboard row. An ROI calculation from a Stage-1 baseline is simulated and
passes; the same calculation presented as a client result is a programme
failure. There is no third state, and the tag is the only thing separating them.

Two scoreboard rows refuse simulation outright — SM-21 paid pilots and SM-22
revenue — because their entire value is that politeness cannot produce them.
Both are expected to read zero through month 06 and possibly well past it.

Real client names, real prices and real contact details live in gitignored
`*.local.md` files. The tracked offer sketch and the tracked case study carry
placeholder identities so this repository can be made public without a scrub,
and `templates/case-study.md` is written that way from the start.
